# HR Analytics – Employee Attrition Analysis

## Project Overview

This project focuses on analyzing employee attrition and workforce trends using Microsoft Excel. The objective is to understand employee turnover patterns, identify groups with higher attrition rates, and explore workplace factors associated with employees leaving the organization.

Using the IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition dataset, I performed data cleaning, data transformation, exploratory data analysis, and dashboard development to generate meaningful HR insights.

## Project Objectives

- Analyze overall employee attrition and calculate the attrition rate.
- Identify departments and job roles with higher employee turnover.
- Examine attrition patterns across different age groups.
- Understand the relationship between overtime and employee attrition.
- Analyze attrition across monthly income groups.
- Explore how work-life balance relates to employee attrition.
- Present key workforce insights through an interactive Excel dashboard.

## Dataset

**Dataset:** IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance

**Source:** [Kaggle – IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition Dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/pavansubhasht/ibm-hr-analytics-attrition-dataset)

- 1,470 employee records
- 35 original columns
- Includes employee demographics, job information, salary, job satisfaction, overtime, and attrition status.

*Note: This is a fictional dataset created for analytics and educational purposes.*

## Project Workflow

1. **Data Understanding:** Examined the dataset structure, columns, and employee information.
2. **Data Cleaning:** Reviewed and prepared the dataset for analysis, documenting the process in a cleaning log.
3. **Data Transformation:** Created additional analytical categories, including age groups and monthly income bands.
4. **Exploratory Data Analysis:** Analyzed employee attrition across different workforce attributes using Excel formulas and calculations.
5. **Dashboard Development:** Created an interactive dashboard with KPI cards, charts, and filters to explore employee attrition patterns.

## Key Insights

The dashboard highlights several notable patterns:

- Overall employee attrition rate is **16.1%**, with 237 employees leaving out of 1,470.
- Sales Representatives show the highest attrition rate among the displayed job roles at **39.8%**.
- The Sales department has the highest department-level attrition rate at **20.6%**.
- Employees under 25 show a comparatively high attrition rate of **39.2%**.
- Employees working overtime have a higher attrition rate (**30.5%**) compared with those not working overtime (**10.4%**).
- The lowest monthly income band (under 3K) has an attrition rate of **28.6%**.

These patterns can help identify workforce segments that may require further investigation and targeted employee retention strategies.

*These are descriptive associations from the dataset and should not be interpreted as proof of causation.*
